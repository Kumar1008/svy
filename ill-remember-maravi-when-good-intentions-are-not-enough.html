“I’ll Remember…” Maravi — When Good Intentions Are Not Enough

  • 1st October 2026

“Neduneer maravi madi thuyil naangum
Keduneerar kaamak kalan.”

— Thirukkural 605

In the previous article, we explored the first of the four ships identified by Thiruvalluvar: Neduneer — postponement.

We saw how an apparently harmless sentence—
“I’ll do it later…”
—can quietly move an important action from the present into an uncertain future.

Arun had found a valuable training programme.

He genuinely wanted to apply.

He needed a recommendation from his teacher, but instead of contacting him immediately, he thought:
“There are still ten days. I’ll ask tomorrow.”

A couple of days later, the application came back to his mind.

This time he remembered what needed to be done.

He thought:
“I must message my teacher. I’ll do it as soon as I get home.”

He reached home.

Other things demanded his attention.

And somewhere between remembering and doing... the intention disappeared.

The first ship had whispered:
“Later…”

Now a second ship had quietly arrived:
“I forgot.”

This is Maravi — மறவி.

Good Intentions Are Not Always Enough

Most of us have experienced something like this.

While cooking, we suddenly remember:
“I need to pay the electricity bill.”

Then the phone rings.

Ten minutes later, the thought has disappeared.

A student remembers an important question to ask the teacher.

“I’ll ask during class.”

The class begins.

The question is forgotten.

We meet a friend and suddenly say:
“I’ve been thinking of calling you for the last three months!”

We genuinely meant to call.

But we did not.

This reveals something important:

Intending to do something and creating the conditions for actually doing it are not the same thing.

A good intention may arise sincerely.

But unless that intention is translated into some form of action, it can disappear as quickly as it appeared.

“I’ll Remember”

There is a small sentence many of us use with great confidence:
“I’ll remember.”

Someone tells us an important date.
“I’ll remember.”

We realise that something must be purchased.
“I’ll remember.”

A task comes to mind.
“I’ll remember.”

We promise ourselves to make a phone call.
“I’ll remember.”

But our minds are continuously receiving new information.

Conversations.
Messages.
Responsibilities.
Emotions.
Notifications.
Unexpected events.
